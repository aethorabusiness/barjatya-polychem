Masterbatch Selection for Blown Film Extrusion: Bubble Stability & COF

Addressing melt fracture, die build-up, coefficient of friction (COF), and barrier properties in monolayer and co-extruded films.

Direct Answer: Blown film extrusion requires masterbatches formulated with carrier resins that match or exceed the melt index of the base LLDPE/LDPE resin, paired with high-dispersion pigments and functional processing aids to prevent bubble instability, die lines, and pinholes.

1. Preventing Die Lines and Gel Defects

In thin-gauge films (15–50 microns), undispersed pigment agglomerates or degraded carrier polymer particles create visible gels and weak pinhole spots. Masterbatches manufactured with dedicated twin-screw compounding ensure complete pigment wetting and filterability.

2. Coefficient of Friction (COF) Management

High-speed form-fill-seal (FFS) packaging machines require precise film slip properties. Combining Slip (erucamide) and Antiblock masterbatches allows film layers to slide smoothly over metal forming collars without blocking on the roll.

3. Polymer Processing Aids (PPA)

When extruding high-viscosity metallocene LLDPE (mLLDPE), fluoropolymer PPA masterbatch dynamically coats the die lips, eliminating melt fracture (sharkskin) and allowing higher line output at lower extruder motor loads.
